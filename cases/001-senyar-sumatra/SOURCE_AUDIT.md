# Source Audit

## Case 001 — Cyclone Senyar and the 2025 Sumatra Floods

**Status:** active source verification

This document records the evidence used to establish the factual basis of Case 001 before scientific analysis begins.

The objective is to prevent unsupported statements, preserve source provenance, identify conflicting or evolving information, and distinguish established facts from hypotheses.

---

# 1. Evidence classes

Sources are grouped according to their role.

## Class A — Primary operational or observational sources

Preferred for event chronology, observations, warnings, cyclone classification, and official impact information.

Examples used in this case:

- BMKG
- IMD / RSMC New Delhi
- BNPB
- Hong Kong Observatory for western North Pacific systems
- official observational datasets

## Class B — Authoritative synthesis

Used when an international or national scientific organisation synthesises information from multiple primary sources.

Example:

- World Meteorological Organization

These sources are highly authoritative but should not automatically replace the underlying primary observation when it is available.

## Class C — Peer-reviewed scientific literature

Used for physical interpretation, methodology, comparison with previous events, and independent scientific analysis.

Peer-reviewed conclusions may inform hypotheses but will not be copied uncritically.

Where practical, Earth in Extremes will independently reproduce the relevant diagnostics.

## Class D — Scientific preprints

Useful for recent analysis and methodological ideas.

Preprints have not completed the full peer-review process and must be explicitly identified as such.

## Class E — Secondary reporting

News media and other secondary reporting may be useful for identifying impacts or locating primary information.

They should not normally form the scientific basis of the analysis.

# 2. Core event chronology

## Precursor disturbance

BMKG reports that Tropical Cyclone Seed 95B had been monitored from **21 November 2025**.

Status:

**Supported by primary source**

Source:

BMKG, post-event reporting on Tropical Cyclone Senyar.

---

## Depression and cyclone period

IMD/RSMC New Delhi archives:

**Cyclonic Storm Senyar over the Strait of Malacca during 25–27 November 2025.**

The archive contains operational products at multiple analysis times, including:

- national bulletins
- RSMC bulletins
- observed and forecast tracks
- quadrant wind graphics
- heavy-rainfall products
- extended-range cyclogenesis outlooks

Status:

**Established**

Primary source:

IMD / RSMC New Delhi 2025 system archive.

---

## Cyclone naming / intensification

BMKG reported that Seed 95B had developed into **Tropical Cyclone Senyar by 07:00 WIB on 26 November 2025**.

At that analysis time BMKG placed the centre near:

\[
5.0^\circ \mathrm{N},\;98.0^\circ \mathrm{E}
\]

with approximately:

- minimum central pressure: **998 hPa**
- maximum wind: **43 kt**
- movement: westward at approximately **10 km h\(^{-1}\)**

Status:

**Established for this specific BMKG analysis time**

Important:

These values must not automatically be treated as Senyar's final best-track maximum intensity.

Operational estimates may differ among agencies because of:

- analysis time
- observing data available
- wind averaging period
- classification procedure
- later post-event reanalysis

A single authoritative best-track convention should be selected before quantitative intensity comparisons are made.

# 3. Historical rarity

Several authoritative descriptions exist and their wording is not identical.

## IMD formulation

IMD's 2025 annual cyclone assessment states that Senyar was:

**the first cyclone in IMD's recorded history since 1891 over the Strait of Malacca.**

Status:

**Established within the scope of the IMD historical record**

This should not automatically be rewritten as:

> the first cyclone ever in the Strait of Malacca.

---

## WMO formulation

WMO describes Senyar as:

**the first known system in the satellite era to reach tropical-cyclone intensity in the Strait of Malacca.**

Status:

**Established authoritative synthesis**

This is the preferred wording when discussing satellite-era rarity.

---

## Working wording for Earth in Extremes

Until historical climatology is independently examined, use:

> Senyar was an exceptionally rare tropical cyclone in the Strait of Malacca. IMD describes it as the first cyclone in its historical record since 1891 in the strait, while WMO identifies it as the first known system in the satellite era to reach tropical-cyclone intensity there.

Do not use:

> first cyclone ever.

# 4. Rainfall observations

## Maximum reported daily rainfall

BMKG's December 2025 climate bulletin reports:

**411 mm day\(^{-1}\)**

at:

**BPP Kuala rain gauge, Bireuen Regency, Aceh**

on:

**26 November 2025**

Status:

**Established BMKG observation**

This is currently the strongest source for the maximum daily Indonesian rainfall value used in this case.

WMO independently reports the same 411 mm daily maximum for Bireuen Regency.

Status:

**Cross-supported by BMKG and WMO**

---

## Additional BMKG observations

BMKG reported extreme rainfall during 25–27 November including:

- Aceh Utara: **310.8 mm day\(^{-1}\)**
- Medan: **262.2 mm day\(^{-1}\)**
- Tapanuli Tengah: **229.7 mm day\(^{-1}\)**
- Padang Pariaman: **154 mm day\(^{-1}\)**

Status:

**Established operational observations**

Important:

These values should not be compared directly without checking:

- station identity
- observation period
- local-day definition
- gauge type
- quality-control status

The 411 mm Bireuen value and the 310.8 mm Aceh Utara value are not treated as contradictory because they refer to different observation locations.

# 5. Background climate state

## Indian Ocean Dipole

BMKG monitoring during November 2025 showed a **negative Indian Ocean Dipole**.

Selected BMKG values include:

Early November:

\[
DMI \approx -1.70
\]

Second dekad:

\[
DMI \approx -0.57
\]

Monthly November value reported later:

\[
DMI \approx -0.83
\]

Status:

**Established**

Important:

The value changed substantially during the month.

Therefore the project should not describe November 2025 using a single DMI value without specifying its averaging period.

Scientific question:

> To what extent did the negative IOD precondition enhanced convection and moisture availability near western Indonesia?

Causality has not yet been established by this case.

# 6. Intraseasonal and equatorial variability

BMKG documented active or predicted activity involving:

- Madden–Julian Oscillation
- equatorial Rossby wave
- Kelvin wave

around the Maritime Continent during November 2025.

BMKG specifically stated that an active **equatorial Rossby wave in the same region as Senyar contributed to enhanced rainfall intensity**.

BMKG also identified overlapping MJO, Kelvin-wave, and equatorial-Rossby-wave influence around parts of:

- Strait of Malacca
- Indian Ocean west of Aceh
- southern Indian Ocean sector near Indonesia

Status:

**Established operational diagnosis**

However:

Earth in Extremes will attempt an independent diagnosis rather than simply reproduce the BMKG interpretation.

Candidate independent diagnostics:

- NOAA OLR
- 850-hPa zonal wind
- 200-hPa zonal wind
- Wheeler–Hendon RMM index
- longitude–time diagrams
- filtered equatorial-wave fields where methodologically justified

# 7. Surrounding tropical systems

## Tropical Cyclone Koto

Hong Kong Observatory records Koto as forming east of the southern Philippines on **24 November 2025**.

Koto entered the South China Sea on 26 November and reached typhoon intensity, with its peak reported on 27 November.

BMKG also reported indirect effects from Koto on parts of northern Indonesia, including rainfall and marine conditions.

Status:

**Koto's concurrent presence is established**

However:

> Koto contributed dynamically to Senyar's formation

is **not established**.

This remains a hypothesis requiring circulation and moisture-transport analysis.

---

## Cyclonic Storm Ditwah

IMD records:

**Cyclonic Storm Ditwah over the southwest Bay of Bengal and adjoining Sri Lanka during 26 November–2/3 December 2025.**

The same IMD operational archive contains products for both Senyar and Ditwah.

Status:

**Concurrent development established**

However:

> Ditwah contributed dynamically to Senyar's formation

is **not established**.

The systems may instead have developed within a broader common large-scale environment.

This distinction will be tested.

---

# 8. Complex regional circulation

IMD's post-event forecast assessment indicates that **multiple vorticity centres were present within the ITCZ environment**, complicating forecast prediction of the eventual genesis location.

Status:

**Supported by authoritative operational assessment**

This is important because it provides evidence that the environment was dynamically complex before Senyar's organisation.

It does **not** prove that every surrounding disturbance contributed to Senyar.

---

# 9. Monsoon environment

BMKG described strengthening Asian monsoon influence and dominant low-level westerly flow across parts of Indonesia during the period.

Status:

**Supported operational assessment**

Candidate analysis:

- 850-hPa wind anomalies
- zonal-wind anomalies
- moisture transport
- convergence
- comparison against November climatology

The magnitude and direct role of the monsoon circulation remain to be quantified independently.

# 10. Impacts

## Indonesia

WMO's later synthesis reports **1,240 deaths in Indonesia** associated with the event.

BNPB maintained a continuously updated disaster-response dashboard during the emergency.

Earlier snapshots contained lower totals because search, rescue, verification, and reporting were still ongoing.

Status:

**Major mortality firmly established**

Important:

Impact numbers must always include an observation or reporting date.

The project must not combine casualty figures from different dates as though they describe the same reporting state.

---

## Regional impacts

AHA Centre reported widespread flooding and landslides affecting:

- northern Sumatra
- Peninsular Malaysia
- southern Thailand

and linked the regional rainfall environment to the combined influence of the northeast monsoon and Senyar's development.

Status:

**Regional disaster extent established**

This does not mean Senyar alone caused all rainfall across the three countries.

# 11. Peer-reviewed scientific literature

**2025 Senyar storm: Malacca vortex as an emerging hazard over Southeast Asia**

Journal:

**Atmospheric Research**

DOI:

`10.1016/j.atmosres.2026.109277`

Status:

**Peer-reviewed research article**

Open access:

**Yes**

Key findings reported by the authors include:

- interaction between MJO and equatorial Rossby activity
- negative IOD as an important environmental precondition
- strong moisture convergence
- unusual near-equatorial cyclogenesis
- substantial ensemble track errors
- underestimation of extreme precipitation by TIGGE ensemble forecasts

Use in Earth in Extremes:

**Major scientific reference**

Important:

The project's independent calculations should not simply reproduce the article's interpretation.

Where feasible, its conclusions should be tested using independently processed open datasets.

# 13. Open data audit

## GPM IMERG

Provider:

NASA / GPM

Current retrospective product:

**IMERG V07**

Relevant characteristics:

- approximately 0.1° spatial resolution
- half-hourly precipitation available
- coverage extends back to 1998
- Final Run suitable for retrospective analysis after latency period

Potential use:

- rainfall evolution
- event accumulation
- spatial distribution
- rainfall persistence
- comparison with BMKG station observations

Status:

**Suitable for reproducible core analysis**

Important limitation:

IMERG is a satellite-based precipitation estimate and must not be treated as equivalent to a rain gauge.

---

## ERA5

Provider:

Copernicus Climate Change Service / ECMWF

Relevant characteristics:

- global
- hourly
- approximately 0.25° atmospheric grid
- available from 1940 onward
- CC BY licensed through the Climate Data Store

Candidate variables include:

### Single levels

- mean sea-level pressure
- 10-m wind
- sea-surface temperature
- total-column water vapour
- total precipitation

### Pressure levels

- wind components
- temperature
- geopotential
- specific humidity
- vertical velocity

Potential derived diagnostics:

- relative vorticity
- divergence
- vertical wind shear
- moisture flux
- integrated vapour transport
- moisture-flux convergence

Status:

**Suitable for reproducible large-scale and synoptic analysis**

Important limitation:

ERA5 is a reanalysis, not an observation.

Its resolution is insufficient to resolve all convective and mesoscale structure associated with Senyar.

---

# 16. Terminology rules

Avoid:

> unprecedented cyclone

unless the reference period and dataset are specified.

Prefer:

> exceptionally rare cyclone in the historical record examined.

Avoid:

> multiple systems caused the Sumatra disaster.

Prefer initially:

> multiple tropical disturbances and large-scale modes were simultaneously active across the surrounding Indo-Pacific region. Their dynamical relationships will be investigated.

Avoid:

> 411 mm fell across Aceh.

Use:

> BMKG recorded a daily total of 411 mm at BPP Kuala in Bireuen Regency on 26 November 2025.

Avoid:

> La Niña and negative IOD caused Senyar.

Use:

> Senyar developed within a background of weak La Niña and negative IOD conditions. Their potential contributions to environmental preconditioning will be evaluated.
